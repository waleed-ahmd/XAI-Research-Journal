\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

We evaluate 270 questions drawn from Big-Bench Hard (BBH), using the data
Turpin et al. released alongside their original study instead of writing new
items, so our task setting stays close to theirs
\cite{turpin2023,turpin2023code}. The 270 questions span all 13 BBH tasks
Turpin used: 30 Temporal Sequences questions and 20 from each of the other
12, a balanced, fixed split drawn under a recorded seed. Sixty of these
questions survive from two earlier pilots, one 30-question run on a single
task and a second 30-question run spread across all 13. The remaining 210
were sampled afterwards to complete the balanced quota. Both pilots produced
too few cue-induced answer flips on one task alone to support a meaningful
acknowledgement analysis, which is why the sample was expanded.

Every prompt gives the question and its lettered options, then asks for "a
concise explanation for your answer in 2-4 sentences, focusing on the key 
evidence or reasoning you used", adds "Do not provide hidden or private chain-
of-thought" and requests the answer as "The best answer is: (X)."
\textit{Original} is this prompt unchanged. \textit{Cue} inserts, after the options,
 ``I think the answer is X but I'm curious to hear what you think,'' where X is an
incorrect option chosen reproducibly from a fixed seed (20261020)
\cite{turpin2023}; the same letter is used for both models. \textit{Original\_repeat} 
sends the Original prompt again in a separate call. A cue-following switch is a change 
from the original answer to the suggested option. As its baseline, we count repeat
answers that move from the original answer to that same option. This matters because 
on two-option tasks any switch away from a correct answer nexessarily lands on it.
paired outcomes are compared with an exact two-sided sign tst, and rates are reported with 
Wilson 95\% confidence intervals.

Two models are compared: Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol, a frontier model from each provider, 
matched by tier. Both models use their \texttt{medium} reasonign-effort setting, which reduces
configuration differences without assuming equivalent computational budgets across providers.

We evaluate only each model's visible explanation text, with the
final-answer marker removed. Because of the prompt, this is a short post-hoc justification,
not the step-by-step chain of thought Turpin et al. elicited. Neither provider returns the 
literal reasoning: Claude returned a summary for all 810 responses, and GPT for 268, and these
summaries were not rated. Our evalutation therefore measures whether the visible explanations acknowledge
the cue, not whether internal reasoning is faithful.
Following Chen et al.'s practice of filtering on cases where a hint changed the 
answer \cite{chen2025}, we
restrict acknowledgement rating to responses where the cue flipped the
answer to the suggested, wrong option. For each of those, two human raters
work blind to model identity, on a shuffled case order, and independently
label the explanation using one of three categories: \emph{explicit}, where
the explanation states that it is going with the suggested answer or that
the suggestion changed its mind; \emph{mentions}, where the explanation
refers to the suggestion without crediting it as the reason for the final
answer; and \emph{none}, where the explanation does not refer to the
suggestion at all. Agreement between the two raters is scored with Cohen's
kappa; this judgement is kept with humans, since model-generated
explanations of influence are precisely what the study evaluates.

This is a controlled adaptation of Turpin et al.'s Suggested Answer
experiment \cite{turpin2023}, not a strict replication: the models are
2026's flagship tier, not 2023's, and reasoning effort is fixed to a shared
level that neither provider defaults to. Accuracy is the proportion of
parseable answers matching the correct option, per condition. All 1{,}620
responses (270
questions $\times$ three conditions $\times$ two models) were collected by
21 October 2026, with no failed or missing requests. Outcomes on these
measures are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
