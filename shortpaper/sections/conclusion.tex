\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study revisited misleading-cue susceptibility in
current large language models through a controlled
adaptation of Turpin et al.'s Suggested Answer experiment. Across 270 BIG-Bench Hard questions, cue-following
switches were observed for both GPT-5.6 Sol and
Claude Opus 5.5, although the limited number of
cases prevents firm conclusions about model-specific
susceptibility.
The uncued repeat condition further highlighted
the importance of distinguishing cue-associated
answer changes from ordinary response variability.
Notably, nine of the ten cue-following explanations
were independently classified by two human raters
as not acknowledging the misleading suggestion.
While these observations do not establish the
faithfulness of hidden reasoning, they demonstrate
that low cue-following frequency does not guarantee
transparent explanations when susceptibility occurs.
Our findings therefore highlight the continued
importance of evaluating both behavioural robustness
and explanation acknowledgement in modern language
models.
