## Follow-up literature review

Following our previous meeting, we looked beyond the original Wei (2022),
Turpin (2023) and Lanham (2023) papers to see how their findings have held
up in more recent research.

Our main question was whether later work has:
- corrected or challenged the original findings;
- reproduced the findings on newer models; or
- changed how researchers measure CoT faithfulness.

## What we found

The original papers have not simply been "corrected", but their findings have
been qualified and challenged by later work.

Turpin et al.'s main observation that CoT can fail to mention factors that
influence an answer has been reproduced on newer reasoning models. However,
recent studies suggest that reasoning models are more likely to verbalise
these influences than earlier models, although they are still not completely
faithful.

Lanham et al.'s findings are more complicated. Later work has questioned the
validity of their simple faithfulness metric because it can be affected by
answer-choice bias and model accuracy. We therefore do not want to simply
repeat Lanham's original scaling experiment.

We also found newer work proposing alternative ways of testing faithfulness,
including interventions on reasoning steps and tests using more realistic
prompts.

## Updated research direction

Based on this literature, we are interested in revisiting the original
faithfulness question using a modern reasoning model rather than simply
reproducing the original experiments.

Our current working question is:

**How does the faithfulness of Chain-of-Thought explanations in modern
reasoning models compare with the behaviour reported in earlier studies?**

This is still a working question and may be narrowed further.

## Possible experiment

We are currently considering a small comparison of two current LLMs:
GPT-5.6 Luna and Claude Sonnet 5.5.

Rather than testing many models and datasets, we plan to use a limited set of
questions based on a task investigated in the earlier faithfulness literature.

Each model would receive the same questions in two conditions:

1. the original prompt;
2. the same prompt with one controlled cue introduced.

We would record the final answer and the model's visible reasoning/explanation,
then examine whether the cue affected the answer and whether the explanation
acknowledged that influence.

We are currently considering approximately 30 questions. This would give us
120 model responses across the two models and two conditions, while keeping
the study small enough to analyse within our page limit.

The exact task, questions, cue and evaluation procedure will be finalised after
a small pilot.

## Scope consideration

We have limited page count. Therefore, we need to keep the experiment small
and focused rather than attempting a broad benchmark or comparison of many
models.
