## Chen et al. literature review — Peter Kennedy

**Entry type:** Source reviewed
**Author:** Peter Kennedy


### What happened

To contribute a new source, I chose and reviewed Chen et al. (2025), *Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think*. It applies Turpin et al.'s paired-prompt method to modern reasoning models. My notes are in [`literature/chen-notes.md`](../literature/chen-notes.md).

### Decisions made

- **Decision:** Add Chen et al. (2025) as a core related-work source.
  - **Why:** Our 30 Sept entry says Turpin's finding has been reproduced on newer reasoning models, but no paper in `literature/` backed that claim up. Chen et al. is the main evidence for it. It uses a sycophancy hint, which is the same type of cue as Turpin's "Suggested Answer" and our planned cue, and it finds that reasoning models acknowledge hints more often than older models but still only in a minority of cases (about 25% for Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 39% for DeepSeek R1). That gives us a 2023 → 2025 → our 2026 comparison line.

- **Decision (proposed, needs group agreement):** Use Chen et al.'s verbalisation criterion as our operational definition of "acknowledged the cue". Under this criterion, the explanation must both mention the cue and depend on it to reach the answer, and a mention only in a final checking step does not count.
  - **Why:** "Is cue acknowledgement judged consistently?" is one of the things our pilot is supposed to check, but we haven't defined it yet. Chen's definition is precise, already used in published work, and close to our setting. Adopting it saves us inventing our own and makes our results easier to compare.

- **Decision:** Do *not* switch our dataset to Chen's MMLU/GPQA.
  - **Why:** On 2 Oct we chose BBH to stay as close as possible to Turpin. Chen's results are useful context, but switching datasets would move us away from our research question. This means Chen's numbers can't be a direct baseline for ours, which we need to say in the paper.

### Pivots, dead ends, disagreements

No pivot this week, but reading Chen raised two tensions the group should discuss:

1. **Comparability.** Our plan compares our results with Turpin. Chen is the closest modern study, but it uses different datasets and an automatic LLM judge. We need to decide how strongly to compare with it: as a direct comparison, or only as context.
2. **Hidden reasoning.** Chen et al. could not test OpenAI's o1/o3 because their full reasoning isn't visible. TJ raised the same question on 29 Sept about current ChatGPT and Claude models. If GPT-5.6 Luna only shows a summary of its reasoning, our acknowledgement measure may not mean the same thing for both models. This could affect our model choice, so it should be checked in the pilot.

I also noted that the paper is a preprint from a model developer evaluating partly its own models. That isn't a reason to reject it, but it is worth stating when we cite it.

### Still open

- [ ] Does the group agree to adopt Chen's verbalisation criterion for judging cue acknowledgement?
- [ ] Can we see the full reasoning of GPT-5.6 Luna and Claude Sonnet 5.5, or only summaries? Check during the pilot.

- [ ] Judge acknowledgement by hand (two members independently, then compare) or with an LLM judge as Chen did? With 120 responses, hand-judging seems feasible.

## Harness model and effort decisions — Luca Knierim

**Entry type:** Decision
**Author:** Luca Knierim

### What happened

Updated the harness config on the `cot-faithfulness-harness` branch: confirmed the OpenAI model id and made both providers' reasoning depth an explicit, recorded config value instead of leaving one implicit. No real model calls were made.

### Decisions made

- **Decision:** Switched the OpenAI model from "GPT-5.6 Luna" (which doesn't exist under that name) to GPT-5.6 Terra (`gpt-5.6-terra`).
  - **Why:** GPT-5.6 Terra is the mid tier of the GPT-5.6 family, which puts both models we're comparing at a comparable mid tier (Claude Sonnet 5.5 is Anthropic's "best combination of speed and intelligence" tier). Confirmed against OpenAI's current model docs rather than guessing.
- **Decision:** Added an explicit `effort` setting for Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`high`) alongside the existing `reasoning.effort` for OpenAI (`medium`), both now recorded in `harness/config.yaml` and included in the resumable-run hash.
  - **Why:** The Claude side was previously relying on the API's own default effort level without us ever stating what that was. Values were set to match current defaults exactly, so this is a documentation/traceability fix, not a behaviour change.
- **Decision (supersedes the two above, same day):** Switched the model pair again, from Claude Sonnet 5.5 / GPT-5.6 Terra to **Claude Opus 5.5 / GPT-5.6 Sol**, both at `medium` effort.
  - **Why:** Preparing the presentation slides surfaced that Sonnet 5.5 and Terra are each a mid/fast tier, not each provider's flagship, which is what "comparable models" should mean for a like-for-like comparison. Opus 5.5 and Sol are the two providers' equivalent flagship tiers. Anthropic also has a tier above Opus (Fable/Mythos) with no OpenAI counterpart, so it's out of scope here. Effort is set to `medium` for both, rather than each provider's own default (which previously gave Claude `high` and OpenAI `medium`), so the comparison also controls for effort level rather than mixing it with the tier choice. This roughly doubles the estimated run cost (~$0.99/provider for the full 90-call run instead of ~$0.5-0.6), which is an accepted trade-off for a cleaner comparison.
