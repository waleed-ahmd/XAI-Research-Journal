## What happened

Read Wei et al. (2022), *Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models*. It's the paper that introduces chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, so it is the starting point for our topic. I took notes that i've placed inside [`literature/wei-notes-tj.md`]

## Reflection
Before reading this paper I wasn't entirely sure what chain of thought thinking even was. I expected the paper to go lower level into how a model might use chain of thought for its own reasoning as opposed to the methodology of supplying a model with chain of thought exemplers in the prompt to draw out perforamnce gains in its reasoning.

The paper lists "an interpretable window into the behavior of the model" as one of the attractive properties of chain-of-thought prompting (p. 3). But in its limitations section it admits that chain of thought "does not answer whether the neural network is actually 'reasoning'" (p. 9), so there's a bit of ambiguity here, especially for me when I first read it. The paper presents interpretability as a benefit whilst also admitting they can't tell whether the steps reflect what the model actually did, or whether the output is just plausible-sounding text. They do address this by calling it an open question (p. 3 and p. 9), and later describe interpretability as "just a side effect" (p. 24), meaning it wasn't their goal. Originally, I interpreted the paper as contradicting itself here, but I now think it's more that the paper offers interpretability as a benefit without actually testing it, so whether chain of thought is a faithful explanation remains open. That matters for us: if CoT is used as an explanation, it could be plausible but unfaithful, and that's what Turpin et al. go on to test.

After reading this, our initial research question from Waleed: "How reliable are chain-of-thought explainations as an explaination method for large language models." stands even more firm because the paper is saying that knowing whether the produced chain of thought explanation is a true and faithful explanation is an open question of chain-of-thought thinking. If this was an open question in 2022, it's worth testing whether current models are any more faithful.
