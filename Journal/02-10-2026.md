## Final Research experimental plan

Following our literature review and discussion with our professor, we have now
narrowed the project to a small re-evaluation of the Chain-of-Thought
faithfulness finding reported by Turpin et al. (2023).

Rather than creating a new benchmark, we will use questions from the same BBH
benchmark investigated in the Turpin study. This allows us to keep the task
setting as close as possible to the original work and focus our comparison on
the change in model behaviour.

## Models

We will evaluate two current LLMs:

- GPT-5.6 Luna
- Claude Sonnet 5.5

The exact model/version and settings used for each run will be recorded so that
the experiment can be reproduced.

## Dataset

We will use **30 questions** taken from the BBH questions and used in Turpin et al.
The same 30 questions will be given to both models.

We will not create new questions for the main experiment.

## Experimental conditions

Each question will be tested in two conditions.

### Condition 1 — Original

The model receives the original Turpin question/prompt without the additional
cue.

### Condition 2 — Cue

The same question is presented with a controlled cue based on Turpin et al.'s
"Suggested Answer" manipulation.

The question itself is otherwise kept unchanged.

This gives us:

    30 questions
        ×
    2 models
        ×
    2 conditions
        =
    120 model responses

## What we will record

For every response we will record:

1. The model's final answer.
2. The model's visible reasoning/explanation.
3. Whether the answer changed between the original and cue conditions.
4. If the answer changed, whether the explanation acknowledged the cue.

We will not make claims about the model's hidden internal reasoning. Our
analysis concerns the reasoning/explanation that is actually available in the
model's response.

## Main measures

We will focus on two main measurements:

### 1. Cue influence

How often did adding the cue change the model's final answer?

### 2. Cue acknowledgement

When the cue changed the answer, how often did the model's visible explanation
acknowledge that the cue had influenced its answer?

The second measure is particularly important because the original Turpin study
showed cases where a bias could influence the answer without being reflected
in the generated explanation.

## Comparison with previous research

We will compare our results with the relevant findings reported by Turpin et
al. (2023).

We will also use later work, including Chua and Evans, as related literature.
We are not using their MMLU benchmark as our main experimental dataset because
our objective is to revisit the original Turpin finding as closely as possible.

## Pilot

Before running the full experiment, we will test the procedure on **5 of the
30 questions**.

The pilot will check whether:

- the cue is being applied consistently;
- the models respond to the same prompt structure;
- answer changes can be identified reliably; and
- cue acknowledgement can be judged consistently.

If the pilot reveals a methodological problem, we will revise the procedure
before completing the main experiment.

If no methodological change is required, the five pilot questions will form
part of the final 30-question dataset rather than being treated as an
additional dataset.

## Scope

The study is deliberately small because the final paper is limited to four
pages, with the fourth page reserved for references. This leaves approximately
three pages for the introduction, related work, methodology, results,
discussion and conclusion.

We therefore prioritise a focused comparison over a large benchmark study.

## Current research question

Our working research question is:

**To what extent do current LLMs exhibit the Chain-of-Thought faithfulness
issues reported by Turpin et al. (2023) when evaluated using the same type of
controlled cue?**
