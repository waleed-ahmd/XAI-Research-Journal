# XAI Assignment 01: Research Journal

- **Group Category:** Explainable AI
- **Group Name:** Group 7
- **Due:** 23 October 2026, 11:59 PM

## Purpose

An auditable, dated record of how your group's thinking developed — topic selection reasoning, sources considered and rejected, pivots, meeting notes, and drafts-in-progress, culminating in the Report. This is process evidence, not a second report.

## Requirements

**Hosting:** A GitHub repository with all team members as contributors, add me added as a collaborator by the end of week 2.

**Structure:** Please use the following, simple convention — a `journal/` folder with one dated Markdown file per entry (e.g., `journal/2026-09-20.md`), plus a `README.md` that stays current with the group's topic, scope, and links to the latest report draft. Groups can use GitHub Issues or a Project board instead of/alongside files if they prefer — the requirement is traceability, not a specific tool.

**Minimum cadence:** At least one substantive entry per week for the duration of the project (a "substantive" entry documents a decision, a source reviewed, a pivot, or a meeting outcome — not just "worked on report today").

**Content expectations per entry:** what was discussed/decided, why, what's still open, and (where relevant) who did what. This last point matters for individual-contribution tracking — see the group-work note below.

**Commit history matters:** because commit authorship is visible, each member should commit their own contributions rather than one person transcribing for the group. This is what makes the journal useful for tracing individual team member contributions, not just whole group.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regularity & timeliness | 25% | Weekly substantive commits across the whole project window, not a backfilled entry the night before submission (commit timestamps will be used to check this) |
| Reflective quality | 30% | Entries explain *reasoning* — why a source was rejected, why the topic scope changed — not just a log of activity |
| Evidence of iteration | 20% | Visible dead ends, pivots, or disagreements resolved — a journal that shows zero course-correction across 6 weeks is a red flag, not a sign of a smooth process |
| Traceability to the Report | 15% | I should be able to trace how specific report sections emerged from journal entries |
| Balanced individual contribution | 10% | Commit authorship reasonably distributed across the group members (see note below on any imbalance will be handled fairly) |

## A Note on Fair Grading of Individuals in Groups

Because all three assessments are group-graded, uneven contribution will be monitored. Two mechanisms work well together to this end:

1. **The journal's commit history** will be used as a rough, checkable signal of who contributed when, noting that this will be used in combination with details in the journal of who did what.

2. **A short, confidential peer-evaluation form**, submitted individually alongside the Report, where each member rates their own and teammates' contribution. Details of this process and a template form you will be required to use can be found as an additional assessment. I reserve the right to adjust an individual's grade (±10-15% of the group grade) if I am satisfied that the both the peer evaluations and the journals point in the same direction.
